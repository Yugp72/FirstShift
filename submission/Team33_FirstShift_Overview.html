Restaurants hire constantly from a workforce that is multilingual and includes many people who learn differently: Deaf and hard-of-hearing, low-vision, people who need more time or a seated station. Yet onboarding is usually one format, one speed, one language: a binder, a shadow shift and a quiz. Managers re-explain the same procedures, and a quiz score can't tell them who is actually ready. A confidently wrong answer about allergens is a guest-safety and liability risk, not just a training gap. The result is slow time-to-floor, uneven readiness, and qualified people screened out before they start.

Our solution

  • “How do you learn best?” Every hire chooses captions, picture steps, read aloud, a seated station or extra time. These are framed as work preferences, never a medical or disability record.
  • Training adapts. Each lesson comes as captioned video, pictures, listen (read aloud) or read. Extra time gives larger text and buttons, slower video and speech, and no timers, ever.
  • Four languages live: English, 中文, 粵語 (Cantonese) and Español, across lessons, quizzes, captions and the UI.
  • Accessible by design: WCAG AAA (7:1) text contrast, keyboard use with focus-trapped dialogs, screen-reader alerts (never only a sound), and translated picture descriptions.
  • One job post, every channel. AI writes 8 posts (job board, classifieds, WeChat, WhatsApp in 中文 / 粵語 / Español, flyer, bilingual, easy-read). Applying takes one tap, with no CV needed.
  • Readiness passport. Managers see what each hire learned, how, and how sure they feel. “Scored 100% but feels unsure” becomes a buddy shift; “confident but wrong on allergens” becomes a double-check. Approval is based on evidence and reaches the hire’s phone in their language.

How we built it with Converge.AI

  • Enter is the whole product: the React app, the firstshift-api edge function, Postgres state, and a single deployed URL. We built and deployed through Enter’s agent and CLI.
  • Enter AI powers the live AI: job posts in 8 channels, grading free-text answers in any language with kind feedback, and translating manager notes into the hire’s language. Every call is cached and has a rubric fallback, so the demo never breaks.
  • Framia produced the training media: the allergy-safety video and step photos. It has no burned-in text by design, so one shoot works in every language and captions come from each lesson’s text. Adding a language means adding text, not re-filming.

Results (prototype, Nov 18)

  • Live and working end to end on Enter: post → apply → train → passport → approve → shift-ready.
  • Cantonese added in one day as data plus translations; the automated end-to-end test passes 14/14 against production.
  • Live AI response times: about 24 s to grade a free-text answer, about 45–50 s for a full 8-channel post set; cached repeats are instant.
  • Accessibility pass shipped: AAA contrast verified on every text colour, focus trap and Esc on all dialogs, visual and screen-reader review alerts.
  • The pipeline shows hire-to-shift-ready in 3.1 days and 75% of steps completed, in demo data. “50% less manager time per opening” is a pilot target, not a result.

What it needs to go live

  • Native-speaker review of 中文, 粵語 and Español content, starting with allergen safety. Cantonese is AI-drafted today and labelled as such.
  • A 4–6 week pilot at Imperial Treasure with real SOPs per role. Measure time-to-shift-ready, manager hours per hire, 90-day retention and allergen incidents.
  • Testing with disabled users: screen-reader, Deaf/hard-of-hearing and low-vision hires, plus a formal WCAG audit.
  • Production hardening: manager SSO, per-location roles, and a privacy and retention policy for preference data.
  • Interview scheduling (plan written) and integrations with the restaurant’s scheduling and payroll tools.
  • Public access: make the Enter project public on a custom domain, then add languages and roles as data (Vietnamese is next).
